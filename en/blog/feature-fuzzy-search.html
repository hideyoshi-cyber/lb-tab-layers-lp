Search that forgives typos: finding saved tabs fast

Sep 24, 2026 · By Hideyoshi Saimoto, CEO of Liquid Block Inc. · 3 min read

PostShareShare

The LB TAB Layers search box looks through the titles, URLs and notes of your saved tabs, Solo Tabs and Today’s History. It forgives a one-letter typo, understands abbreviations such as “ytb” for YouTube, and puts the best matches first.

What it searches

Type in the search box and your saved tabs, Solo Tabs and Today’s History are filtered at the same time. Results are split into matching groups (folders) and matching sites.

How it matches

You typeIt finds
google“Google” first, then “Google Drive”, then pages that only mention Google in the URL
gogleGoogle — one wrong letter is fine for words of 4 letters or more
ytbYouTube — letters in order, close together
drive googlePages that contain both words, in any order
invoiceA tab whose note mentions “invoice”, even if the title doesn’t

Exact matches beat matches at the start of a title, which beat matches at the start of a word, which beat matches in the middle. Upper and lower case, and full-width and half-width characters, are treated the same.

Google suggestions

While you type, Google search suggestions appear too. If a page isn’t among your saved tabs, you can jump straight to a web search.

Search by meaning with AI search

When the words you remember aren’t in the title — “the slides for next week’s meeting” — use AI search (PRO, and during the free trial).

Practical examples

Video production: finding a reference by a half-remembered name

You saved a tutorial about masking weeks ago but can’t recall the title. Type “mask” and the matching titles come first. If you only remember the site, an abbreviation such as “ytb” still finds YouTube pages, because the letters appear in order and close together.

Research: combining two words

Typing “battery review” returns only pages that contain both words, in any order, across the title, URL and note. Add a third word to narrow the list further. Every extra word must match, so the results get shorter as you type, not longer.

Freelancing: searching by the note you wrote

If you add a note like “invoice due Friday” to a client page, you can search for “invoice” weeks later and find it even though the page title says something else. It is the quickest way to make old client work easy to find.

Students: surviving typos under time pressure

Typing fast before a deadline, you may write “wikpedia” or “gogle”. For words of four letters or more, one wrong, missing, extra or swapped letter is accepted, so the search still returns what you meant.

Tips and limits

  • Typo tolerance is one letter. Two mistakes in the same word will usually not match. Typo matching applies to words of four or more letters and digits.
  • Abbreviations need the right start. The first letter should begin a word, and the letters should stay close together. “ytb” works for YouTube; a random scatter of letters does not.
  • Several words mean AND. Separate words with a space and every word must appear somewhere in the title, URL or note.
  • It searches your own data. The search covers saved tabs, Solo Tabs and the history list. It does not search the full text of the web pages themselves.
  • Notes count, but weigh less. A match in the title ranks above a match in a note, so a word in both places comes first.
  • Free and PRO. Basic search is available in the free version. AI search, which finds pages by meaning, is a PRO feature. History is also a PRO feature, so the free version has no history list to search.
  • Google suggestions need a network connection. They are fetched while you type; searching your saved tabs does not depend on them.

Frequently asked questions: more about search

Frequently asked questions

Q. Does search work if I make a typo?

Yes. For words of four letters or more, one wrong, missing, extra or swapped letter is fine — “gogle” finds Google.

Q. What does the search box look through?

The titles, URLs and notes of your saved tabs, plus Solo Tabs and Today’s History.

Q. Does the search look inside the text of web pages?

No. It matches the title, the URL and your note for each saved tab. If the words you remember appear only in the page body, try AI search (PRO), or add a note next time you save the page.

Q. Why did my two-word search return fewer results?

Words separated by a space are combined with AND, so a tab must contain every word to appear. Remove a word to widen the list.

Q. Is search the same in the free version and PRO?

The typo-tolerant search box is available in both. AI search and browsing history are PRO features, which are also available during the free trial.
