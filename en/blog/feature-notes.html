Notes: write down why you saved a tab

Sep 24, 2026 · By Hideyoshi Saimoto, CEO of Liquid Block Inc. · 3 min read

PostShareShare

Notes let you attach a short description to a saved tab. Add one from the right-click menu, and it appears as [Note] when you hover over the tab. Writing down why you kept a page saves you from guessing later.

How to add a note

  1. Right-click a saved tab in the side panel.
  2. Choose the notes item (the one with the notepad icon), type your note and save it.
  3. Hover over the tab to see the note, shown as [Note] together with the URL.

In the dashboard, click Add a note… on a tab card to write one right there.

Examples of useful notes

  • “Reference for the quote. Price list is near the bottom.”
  • “Past work for Client A — use the diagram on page 3 in the proposal.”
  • “Log in with SSO. Ask accounting for the ID.”
  • “Reply by next Friday.”

Practical examples

Video production: remembering which reference is for which shot

A motion designer saves a dozen reference pages for one commercial. A note such as “Camera move for shot 3, watch from 0:40” or “Color palette only, ignore the layout” tells you exactly what to take from each page. When the client sends revisions two weeks later, you don’t have to reopen every tab to remember.

Research: separating sources you trust from sources you doubt

When you gather articles for a report, add a few words about each: “Primary source, cite this”, “Opinion piece, don’t quote” or “Numbers differ from the official site.” Later, search also looks through notes, so typing “cite” brings back the sources you marked.

Freelancing: client logins and deadlines

For a client project, keep the shared drive, the staging site and the invoice page in one group. A note on each (“Use the shared account, not mine”, “Send the invoice by the 25th”) turns the group into a small project brief. If you hand the work to someone else, copy the group as Markdown and the notes go with the links.

Students: why you kept the page

Write the question you were trying to answer: “Explains the second proof”, “For essay paragraph 3”. When exam week arrives, a list of links with one-line reminders is far more useful than a list of titles you no longer recognize.

Tips and limits

  • Keep notes short. A note is a reminder of why you saved the page, not a document. For longer writing, use the separate Notes mode (a notepad with auto-save, in the mode bar). It is a different feature from the note on a saved tab.
  • Write the reason, not the title. The title is already visible. “Compare with the Q3 version” adds information; “Pricing page” does not.
  • Notes live on saved tabs. They are not added to Chrome’s own bookmarks, because Chrome bookmarks have no field for them. If you rely on the browser’s bookmark manager alone, the notes will not appear there.
  • Edit later from the same place. The right-click item is called Edit note, and in the dashboard you click the note on a card to change it.
  • Notes help search. The search box matches note text, though a match in the title ranks higher than a match in a note.
  • Free and PRO. The free version allows up to 5 groups (the automatic “frequently used” group is not counted), and notes belong to the tabs you save in them. Stars and tags on bookmarks in the dashboard are limited separately in the free version, and the dashboard itself is a PRO feature.

Frequently asked questions: more about notes

Frequently asked questions

Q. Can I add a note to a saved tab?

Yes. Right-click a saved tab in the side panel and choose the notes item (notepad icon). The note appears as [Note] when you hover over the tab.

Q. Are notes saved in my bookmarks?

No — Chrome bookmarks have no field for notes, so they are kept by the extension. You can back them up with the Google Sheets export or a JSON export.

Q. Is there a difference between a note on a tab and the Notes mode?

Yes. A note on a tab is a short description attached to one saved page. The Notes mode is a notepad for free-form writing that saves automatically. They are separate features and do not share content.

Q. Can I search for text inside my notes?

Yes. The search box looks through titles, URLs and notes. A word that appears only in a note still finds the tab, though title matches are ranked higher.

Q. Will my notes appear in my Chrome bookmarks?

No. Chrome bookmarks have no field for notes, so they are not included there. They are included when you copy as Markdown and when you export to Google Sheets or JSON.
