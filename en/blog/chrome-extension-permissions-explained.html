Tab manager extension permissions explained: what each one is for

Oct 25, 2026 · Liquid Block Inc. · 7 min read

PostShareShare

A permission is a promise about what an extension can do, not proof of what it does. Judge a tab manager by asking whether each permission has a clear reason tied to a feature you can see, and where your data goes. Below is every permission LB TAB Layers requests, with the feature that uses it.

When you add a tab manager to Chrome, a box pops up with warnings like “Read and change all your data on all websites” or “Read and change your browsing history.” It is supposed to make you stop and think. Most people click through. This article is about the moment in between: what those lines mean, and how to tell whether an extension really needs them.

What a permission actually is

Chrome extensions can’t touch your tabs, bookmarks or web pages unless they declare it in a file called the manifest. Chrome reads that file and turns the riskier entries into the warnings you see at install time. Two things follow from this:

  • A permission is a capability, not an action. “Can read your history” doesn’t mean the extension uploads your history. It means it is allowed to look.
  • A tab manager needs more than you might expect. Listing your open tabs, saving them and reopening them already takes several permissions. That is normal. What matters is whether the rest have a reason.

Four questions to ask about any extension

  1. Does every permission match a feature I can see? If you can’t find the feature, ask why the permission is there.
  2. Where is my data stored? On your computer, in Chrome’s own storage such as bookmarks, or on the developer’s server?
  3. What leaves my computer, and when? A good privacy policy names the servers and says what is sent.
  4. Who makes it, and is it maintained? Check the developer name, the update date and the support contact on the Chrome Web Store page.

The big one: “Read and change all your data on all websites”

This warning comes from host access to all sites. It is the line that worries people most, so here is exactly what LB TAB Layers does with it:

  • Overlay mode. A small script runs on ordinary web pages so the sidebar can slide in over the page when you choose the Overlay display mode (PRO, also in the trial).
  • RSS feeds. The same script looks for feed links in the page header, so the panel can offer to follow a site.
  • AI questions about a page. When you ask the AI sidebar about the page you are on, the script reads the page text or your selection — only at that moment.
  • Screenshots and checks. Capturing a page and checking whether saved links still open both need access to those sites.

Recording a tab uses Chrome’s own screen-sharing dialog, so you always pick what is recorded.

What leaves your computer

Your groups and tabs are stored in Chrome’s bookmarks, not on our server, and there is no account to create. These are the network requests the code makes:

  • License check: a random install ID is sent to our server (liquid-block.com) to confirm whether PRO is active. No name, no email, no tab data.
  • AI: in the free version, AI features use the AI built into Chrome. If you add your own API key in PRO, requests go directly to the provider you chose.
  • Search suggestions: while you type in the search box, the text is sent to Google’s suggestion service to show web suggestions.
  • Weather in the clock: an approximate location based on your time zone (not GPS) is sent to a weather service.
  • Link checks, feeds and thumbnails: requests go straight to the sites themselves.
  • Feedback: only if you fill in and send the feedback form.

The full details are in our privacy policy.

How to check permissions yourself

  1. Open chrome://extensions and click Details on any extension.
  2. Read the Permissions list and the Site access setting.
  3. If you only want an extension active on certain sites, change Site access to On specific sites or On click. Some features may stop working, which also tells you what the access was for.

Nobody should install an extension on trust alone. If something on our list doesn’t make sense to you, the support address on the store page reaches the people who wrote the code. For where the data itself sits, see where Chrome bookmarks are stored.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Why does a tab manager need access to all websites?

Usually for features that work inside the page, such as showing a sidebar over it or taking a screenshot of it. In LB TAB Layers, that access is used for the Overlay display mode, RSS feed detection, reading a page when you ask the AI about it, screenshots and dead-link checks.

Q. Does LB TAB Layers upload my tabs or browsing history?

No. Groups and saved tabs are stored as Chrome bookmarks, and Today’s History is read on your computer only. The license check sends a random install ID to our server, not your tabs.

Q. Can I limit which sites an extension can access?

Yes. Open chrome://extensions, click Details and change Site access to On click or On specific sites. Features that need page access, such as screenshots, may then stop working on other sites.

Q. Do I need a Google account connection to use LB TAB Layers?

No. There is no sign-up. The Google account connection is only for the optional Google Sheets sync in Settings.
